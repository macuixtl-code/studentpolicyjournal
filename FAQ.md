# FAQ
### I’m graduating, can I still submit?
Yes! We welcome submissions from graduating and recently graduated students.
 
### I’m a USC student, but not at Price. Can I submit?
Yes! We welcome submissions from outside Price, provided they meet the submission guidelines above.
 
### Can I submit co-authored work?
Yes of course! We expect it, recognizing that much of the work that USC students do is in group projects, and welcome that collaboration.
 
### I’m a faculty member, can I submit?
Yes! Although our primary focus is on publishing student work, we’d be excited to receive submissions from faculty as well.
 
### How long does this all take?
The editing process will take at least 4-5 weeks if things go very smoothly, but many pieces will require more than one round of edits, and each round adds a few weeks. Once pieces are ready for publication, they’ll go into our queue for publication and go out in our next issue.
 
### When can I withdraw a submission?
You can withdraw submissions at any point in our process—we won’t publish anybody against their will. If you do decide to withdraw, we ask that you let us know as soon as possible to avoid unnecessary effort on the part of our staff.
 
### What happens if I take longer than two weeks to respond to edits?  
We’re willing to work with authors who need flexibility, but priority in our editing process may be given to other pieces and publication may be delayed if the timelines we set out in these guidelines aren’t met. We encourage any author in danger of missing a deadline for submitting revisions to reach out to our editorial team.
 
### Are citations expected even for short-form opinion submissions?
Yes. Although many purveyors of opinion work in the policy space do not require academic levels of citation, we require all submissions to adhere to the same standard.
 
### What do you mean by “substantially drafted by AI”?
AI tools are powerful and increasingly effective tools in policy research, writing, and analysis. We do not object to authors using AI for surfacing sources, nor do we mind authors using AI to polish their writing. With that said, we’re excited about publishing your work, not the work of the leading AI labs, so please, write the first draft yourself. When using AI to edit and improve their writing, we expect our authors to do so in a targeted and intentional way that preserves their unique voice, not to upload their manuscript and ask an LLM to rewrite it better. We expect any data analysis to be performed by you, not an AI making choices that aren’t transparent. 
 
All submissions will be screened with AI detection software. If your submission is flagged as containing any AI-written language, we’ll look to the references section for a description of the AI assistance used in the project. Depending on the description provided and the extent of AI use detected, the editorial team may reach out to discuss the AI use in the project. Detection of any AI use without acknowledgement may result in submission rejection.
 
If you have any questions about whether your use falls within these boundaries, please reach out to the editorial team.
 
### I’ve never done this before—where do I start?
We’re looking to publish three broadly defined types of work:
- Academic journal-style articles;
- Think tank-style issue briefs and policy analysis, and 
- Fact-driven opinion work. 
Each of these forms has a reasonably well-defined structure that we encourage submitters to follow in the interest of submitting high-quality, well-organized work. We’ve collected below a non-exhaustive list of journals, think tanks, and news organizations that put out consistently high-quality work worth emulating. 
 
### Journal-Style Articles: 
Policy work in academic journals comes in a few different forms including but not limited to: quantitative and quantitative analysis of policies and programs, analysis of dynamics that impact policymaking and governance structures, and analysis of the information that feeds into policy processes. These articles mostly have a defined structure that we’re all familiar with: Introduction > methodology > results > discussion > conclusion.
 
- Journal of Policy Analysis and Management
- Public Administration Review
- Health Affairs
- Journal of American Public Health
- Cityscape
 
### Think Tank-Style Reports: 
Think tanks publish a variety of kinds of work at various levels of formality without the rigid consistency of structure seen in academic journal articles. We’re looking for work in the vein of the issue briefs and research reports (and opinion, but more on that later) that think tanks publish. One important difference to note: we expect all of our authors to adhere to academic citation standards—think tanks typically don’t do so
 
- The Brookings Institution
- The RAND Corporation
- Pew Research Center
- The Urban Institute
- The Cato Institute
- Center on Budget and Policy Priorities
 
### Fact-Driven Opinion:
These pieces don’t have a consistent form but should marshal evidence toward a conclusion about policy or policymaking. The think tanks and journals above mostly publish opinion pieces in addition to their informational work, often labeled as ‘commentary’, ‘blog posts’, or ‘perspectives’. Major news organizations also publish evidence-driven opinion work worth emulating, but it’s often mixed with more ideologically driven work that we’re less interested in publishing.
